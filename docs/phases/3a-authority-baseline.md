# Phase 3A — Authority Baseline

## Why it was required

The repository contained multiple Product generations, historical decisions, dialogue, and partially superseded mechanisms. The baseline was required so a fresh actor could identify one current Product and architecture without reconstructing chronology or promoting implementation evidence into authority.

## Authority census and reconciliation

The census covered the Product vision and requirements, decisions C-000–C-017, detailed platform topics, Phase-3 architecture, research, current code/evidence, and unresolved review findings. It classified each source as current, refined, superseded, deferred, rejected, evidence, or history. Conflicts were resolved by explicit supersession, never date or file count.

Ratified Product meaning and structural invariants survived. Stale mechanisms—including primary-runtime assumptions, universal wrappers/services, generic workflow domains, mutable latest authority, and framework-owned Product truth—did not. Mechanism was separated from semantic ownership throughout.

## Supersession law

Current canonical documents own present truth. Detailed references own only their named technical surface. Qualification Evidence may strengthen or falsify a mechanism claim but cannot silently create Product authority. Research is comparative Evidence only. Git history reconstructs deeper review chronology.
